# House style

These books are read aloud at bedtime to children aged three to six, on a colour e-ink tablet.
Every book has the same bones so that a child knows what to expect.

## Shape of a book

- Twelve pages plus a cover. Page 12 is always the sleeping page: the main character is asleep,
  and the last words say goodnight.
- One main character the child follows through the whole book. Other characters appear once each.
- A refrain: a short phrase that comes back on several pages ("Goodnight, hens," says Pip. Shh,
  sleepy time. Then, quiet.). Not on every page; roughly every second or third page.
- A journey through time: the light changes across the book, from the day's last warmth to night.
  The sky arc says what that looks like for this book.
- Pages near the end slow down: fewer words, quieter beats, the character getting sleepy.

## Page text

- Final copy, drawn by the reader app in a white panel over the picture. One or two sentences,
  at most about 25 words. Short sentences read aloud well.
- Plain words a four-year-old knows. Rhythm and repetition over rhyme. Counting, sounds
  (drip, drip, drip), and lists of three are welcome. No rhyme forced at the cost of sense.
- Warm, calm, safe. Nothing frightening, no peril, no sadness that isn't resolved on the same page.
- The text names the animal or thing on the page, so the picture and the words agree.

## Image beats

- One beat per page: a single clear picture a child can read at a glance. Say what is in the frame,
  where the main character is, and what the light is doing. Ten to twenty words.
- Secondary animals get their natural colours and a short description. Say how many.
- Say when the main character is not in the picture, or is small and far away.
- The beat is for the illustrator, not the child. It never contains the page text.

## Panel position

- `bottom` by default. `top` when the interesting part of the picture is low in the frame (a den
  under roots, a pond, a basket) or the sky is the quiet part. Roughly one page in three is `top`,
  never two `top` pages in a row at the start, and the sleeping page is usually `bottom`.

## Pictures on e-ink

- Flat, simple shapes and a limited palette of five muted colours plus cream read best.
- No large dark areas: night skies are pale lavender or indigo, never black. Dark colours only
  in outlines and small accents.
- The quarter of the picture where the panel goes stays plain.
- No text, letters or numbers anywhere in a picture.

## Learning-to-read set

- Ten books, cumulative. Each page teaches one new word, shown big above the sentence. Every word
  on a page must already be taught, on that page or earlier in the series, or be one of the book's
  listed sight words.
- Names are decodable and get the big-word slot the first time they appear.
- Pages 10 and 11 use only known words. Page 12 is a nap.
- Pictures show only what the sentence says, on plain cream, with at most one prop.
